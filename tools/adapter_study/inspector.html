Follow a shirt through the model

  1. Person and garment photos
  2. Find clothing pixels
  3. Encode the two references
  4. Refine the image grid three times
  5. Check hands and crop
  6. Restore the room and save

A human parser outlines the old shirt. WeFit hides its design in a working copy. A variational autoencoder converts the photos to a grid of numbers. The Klein transformer refines that grid, guided by the garment and a saved instruction. The decoder turns the result into pixels.

Which inputs shaped the final update?

The final layer receives 4,096 numbers at each image patch. This curve orders those inputs by their estimated contribution on clothing pixels from 14 training photographs. The estimate uses both the input strength and the magnitude of the weights it connects to.

Follow one connection

Click a patch on the feature map or move the slider. A weight is a stored coefficient. An activation is the number the photo produces at that patch. Their product is this input’s contribution to one of 128 outputs at the final step. Each output adds contributions from 4,096 inputs.

What these measurements cover

Klein uses dense layers. Reading a weight, observing a large change at a block and finding that removal improves the result are three different measurements. The study records the changes at all 32 transformer blocks, final-layer channel estimates and held-out image outcomes. It cannot count “useful weights” across nine billion parameters or establish physical garment sizing.
